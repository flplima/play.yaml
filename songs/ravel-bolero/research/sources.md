# Sources: Ravel, Boléro

- **Nicolas Sceaux, LilyPond edition of the full score (2016)**:
  <https://github.com/nsceaux/Ravel_Bolero>, also on IMSLP as file #421137 with its parts.
  The repository is under CC0 1.0: the editor gives it to the public domain.
  It is typeset from the Durand first edition. Used for **all the notes**,
  read as text by a throwaway script in a scratch folder. LilyPond itself was
  never run on it. Trust: high, with two slips found against the print (see
  the README). Not committed.

- **Durand full score, plate D. & F. 11 839 (1929), scan on Wikimedia Commons**:
  <https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:IMSLP01578-Ravel_-_Bolero_Full_Score_Durand_1929.pdf>
  Marked public domain on Commons. The scan carries a later co-edition notice,
  so it is a reprint from the 1929 plates. Used as the reference to check the
  Sceaux edition by eye: both themes, the ostinatos, rehearsals 8, 16 and 18
  and the last bars. Trust: high, it is the edition Ravel saw through the press.
  Not committed.

- **IMSLP, Boléro work page**:
  <https://imslp.org/wiki/Bol%C3%A9ro_(Ravel,_Maurice)>
  The list of editions, the plate numbers, the tempo marking and the pointer
  to the Sceaux sources. Context only.

## Copyright facts

- Ravel died on 28 December 1937. With the French wartime extensions his works
  entered the public domain in France in May 2016.
- The heirs of the set designer Alexandre Benois claimed to be co-authors of
  Boléro, which would have extended the term. The court in Nanterre rejected
  the claim in June 2024.
- The score was first published by Durand in 1929. US works published in 1929
  entered the public domain on 1 January 2025.
