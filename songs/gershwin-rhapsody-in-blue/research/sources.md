# Sources: Rhapsody in Blue

- **IMSLP work page**:
  <https://imslp.org/wiki/Rhapsody_in_Blue_(Gershwin,_George)>
  Lists every edition with its date and IMSLP's copyright tag. Used to pick
  an edition that is both from 1924 and marked public domain in the US.
  The Grofé orchestrations listed there (1926, 1942) were not used.

- **Two-piano score, New York: T.B. Harms, 1924**, IMSLP #10970:
  <https://imslp.org/wiki/Special:ImagefromIndex/10970>
  41 pages. The first page reads "Rhapsody In Blue for Piano and Orchestra",
  with the band reduced to a second piano. IMSLP marks it public domain.
  The scan has no title page or copyright line, so the 1924 date is IMSLP's.
  Primary source: bars 1 to 20, and the split between the solo and the
  second piano in bars 19 and 20. Trust: high for the notes, but the scan of
  page 1 is faint and blotted, so many chords there were checked against the
  edition below.

- **Piano solo, Harms, copyright 1927** (Chappell reprint, plate 31647),
  IMSLP #891278:
  <https://imslp.org/wiki/Special:ImagefromIndex/891278>
  31 pages. Published before 1930, so public domain in the US, and Gershwin's
  70 years are over. IMSLP marks it public domain. Its bars 1 to 15 match the
  1924 second piano note for note and system for system; bars 16 to 18 show
  the same shapes. For bars 1 to 20 it is a reading aid only, to measure
  pitches where the 1924 scan could not be read. For the love theme
  (Andantino moderato, page 20) it is the only source. Trust: high, clean
  engraving.

Both PDFs were downloaded to a scratch folder outside the repo for reading
and deleted afterwards. Only the transcription is committed.

## Copyright facts

- Rhapsody in Blue was first published in 1924. US works published that year
  entered the public domain on 1 January 2020.
- George Gershwin died on 11 July 1937, so the work is also free in countries
  where copyright ends 70 years after the author's death.
- Ferde Grofé's orchestrations (1926 and 1942) are separate works and were
  not used.
