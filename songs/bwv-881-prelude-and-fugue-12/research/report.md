# BWV 881 — research report

Researched 2026-08-06.

## The work

**Prelude and Fugue No. 12 in F minor, BWV 881**, from *The Well-Tempered
Clavier*, Book II (1742). Both movements are in **2/4** — the prelude galant
and song-like, the fugue in **three voices**.

## Sourcing, and why it took two sources

No single freely-licensed machine-readable text of both movements was found.
The Mutopia Project has no BWV 881; CPDL returns 403 to scripted requests;
MuseScore.com is behind Cloudflare; IMSLP's only engraving files for this work
are of the **fugue**. So the two movements come from two places:

- **Fugue** — Kyle Rother's LilyPond edition (IMSLP #497295), typeset from the
  1866 Bach-Gesellschaft with reference to the Henle and Bärenreiter urtexts
  and Alfred Dürr's critical commentary, released with "no rights reserved".
  Rendered to MIDI with LilyPond 2.26 and imported: **1118 notes, exact**.
  Its own header confirms F minor, 2/4, an eighth-note `\partial` pickup, and
  "in 3 voices".
- **Prelude** — Yo Tomita's sequence of Book II. Tomita is a Bach scholar at
  Queen's University Belfast; his MIDI set was long linked from jsbach.net. The
  live host now returns 500, so the file was recovered from the Internet
  Archive (capture of 2000-08-24). **1473 notes**, 2/4, F minor, and nearly
  gridded already — mean deviation 0.0011 beat from a sixteenth grid.

## The cross-check

Both sources contain the fugue, which makes them checkable against each other.
Tomita's copy sits an eighth-note pickup later on the timeline; aligned at
−1.5 beats, **1116 of 1118 notes match position and pitch exactly (99.8%)**.
The remainder are ornaments Tomita realises as written-out notes and Rother
leaves as symbols. Two unrelated editions agreeing that closely is the
strongest evidence available here that the text is right.

## What the previous scores got wrong

Both `examples/bwv881.play.yaml` (headed "Auto-generated by
scripts/csv-to-play.js — multitrack melody reduction") and this song file:

- **Spelled F minor in sharps** — `g#4`, `a#4`, `d#5` where the key has
  A♭, B♭, E♭. Enharmonically the same pitches, but it is not the piece's text.
- **Declared no meter**, so a 2/4 movement compiled in 4/4 and every bar line
  fell in the wrong place.
- Ran a few dozen bars against the real 140 (prelude) and 85 (fugue), and the
  material did not correspond to either movement bar for bar.

## Verification

Fugue: `play-yaml-import --verify` → 1118 → 1118, 0 missing, 0 extra, 0 moved.
Prelude: 1473 → 1473, 0 missing, 0 extra, 20 moved by ≤ 0.0208 beat under
`--snap 16` — a third of a sixteenth, and the jitter is the sequence's, not a
transcription decision.
